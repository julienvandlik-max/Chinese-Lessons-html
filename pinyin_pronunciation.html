Initials are the consonant sounds that start a syllable — 21 of them, plus syllables that start with no consonant at all (covered under Finals).

The distinction English speakers struggle with isn't voiced vs. voiceless — it's unaspirated vs. aspirated: whether a puff of air follows the consonant. Hold your hand in front of your mouth and compare 爸 bà (no puff) with 怕 pà (strong puff) to feel it.

The 6 aspiration pairs

Finals are everything after the initial — the vowel (or vowel + nasal ending) that carries the tone. Some finals stand alone as a whole syllable (spelled with a leading y/w — shown here as the demo).

Watch for the buzzed "i". After zh ch sh r z c s, the letter "i" isn't "ee" — it's a buzzed, vowel-less continuation of the consonant (like the "rr" in 十 shí). Apple's TTS often mispronounces this as "ee" — trust your own ear on real recordings over the audio button for these.
